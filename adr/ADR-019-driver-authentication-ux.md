# ADR-019: Driver Authentication UX (closes OPEN-001)

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-08-25
- Human approval: explicit owner decision, taken sub-decision by sub-decision against the DES-001 package. `D-08`'s grace-expiry write boundary was a fourth property added later, decided by the owner during independent review of this ADR and recorded in "`D-08` — where the line falls at grace expiry, and the two bounds on the number". `D-16` was likewise added later, raised by security review of this ADR and decided by the owner.
- Closes: `OPEN-001 Authentication UX`
- Does not close: `OPEN-002 Android sync terminal-error policy`

## Context

`OPEN-001` has gated production identity work since it was recorded: backend `T017` (OPEN-001 closure) and `T018` (authentication/session), and every production authentication and device-trust flow on the Android client. ADR-015 deliberately specified only the platform mechanisms the client would use once the decision was taken — Android Keystore, biometric-gated secrets — and explicitly did not decide the flow. ADR-014 likewise rethrows `AuthenticationException` and `AccessDeniedException` rather than mapping them, and records that the mapping waits on `OPEN-001`.

`OPEN-001` is not one decision. The `DES-001` Product/UI/UX lane established that it is fifteen coupled sub-decisions, and produced a decision-ready package for them: `ai/design/mobile/auth/` on branch `feat/DES-001-mobile-auth-ux` (PR #4), independently reviewed and **APPROVED**. That package separates accepted facts (`F-`), platform facts (`PF-`), assumptions (`A-`) and canonical silences (`S-`), lays out the options per sub-decision with their cost to a driver in a truck, and marks its own recommendation as a proposal that cannot close anything.

This ADR is the human owner's decision on all fifteen. It was taken one sub-decision at a time, with the options, the designer's recommendation and the business/security trade-off presented for each.

The canonical silences this ADR addresses are real: `S-01` through `S-16` record that no canonical document names a login identifier, a proof mechanism, a password rule, a PIN policy, a session duration, an offline window, a rate-limit value, a recovery policy, a delivery channel or a language moment. **This ADR fills the mechanisms and deliberately leaves several of the numbers open** — see "Deliberately left open", which is the authoritative list. Nothing here contradicts canonical material; it supplies what canonical material never stated.

## Decision

All fifteen sub-decisions are settled as follows. Option letters refer to `ai/design/mobile/auth/05-open-001-decision-alternatives.md`. `D-16` is a **sixteenth** row and was **not** part of the original fifteen: it was raised by security review of this ADR and decided by the owner. Its provenance and reasoning are recorded below the table, as `D-08`’s fourth property is.

### `D-08` — where the line falls at grace expiry, and the two bounds on the number

The fourth property in the `D-08` row is a **decision**, not a proposal. `03-offline-boundaries.md` §2 marks the `GRACE_EXPIRED` row's capture and business-write cells **PROPOSAL** and says the dividing line between the two is the ADR's to draw — *"what must not happen is drawing no line at all"*. This ADR draws it at **the queue**: plain capture is not a business write; an operation that enters the sync queue for the office is. The `PROPOSAL` tag **on the `GRACE_EXPIRED` row** is discharged by this decision rather than carried forward. `03` §2 carries the identical *"Proposed: yes"* / *"Proposed: no new writes"* pair on the `SESSION_REJECTED` row as well, and this ADR does **not** decide that one: `D-09` names session rejection as a re-authentication trigger and never draws its write boundary.

**Who decided this, and when.** The boundary was **not** part of the original fifteen. It was raised during independent review of this ADR, put to the programme owner, and **decided by the owner** — first left to stand without limit, then reversed by them once the consequence below was set beside it. It is recorded with its actor because a decision written without the party who took it is precisely the defect that produced the first draft of this section, in which the Orchestrator stated the design package's proposal as settled fact and the Independent Reviewer caught it.

**The alternative that was rejected, and why.** The position taken first was to let the device keep accepting business writes without limit after expiry. The owner reversed it once the consequence was put alongside it: every request is authorized server-side on arrival and the client may not evaluate that locally, so a driver on a revoked or suspended membership could keep working for days or weeks and have **everything written after expiry** rejected on reconnect. That trades a bounded client-side stop for an unbounded server-side rejection whose handling is `OPEN-002`, which is unresolved. The client-side stop is what keeps the failure visible to the driver at the moment it happens instead of weeks later.

**The citations for that sentence, at the strength they actually carry.** `F-06` fixes the resolution *order* — `Authentication → Principal → Company Context → RBAC → business authorization` — and not, by itself, a frequency. What carries *every* and *on arrival* is `F-07`, whose source requires repository contracts to apply company scope on every tenant-owned query, together with `F-20`, under which an item is `SYNCED` only after backend confirmation, and `F-21`, under which the server evaluates each queued operation individually. That the client may not evaluate authorization locally is `03` §1 point 3, which that file tags **`DERIVED`** from `F-17` (*"business rules are never pushed into the frontend"*) and expressly invites a challenger to attack; it is cited here as the derived claim it is, not as a flat fact.

Note that this argument runs on **per-request authorization**, not on session revocation — and note exactly how far that carries, because the unqualified form of the sentence is false. It is **not** the claim that the server rejects a write for having been authored after a client-side timer elapsed: the server has no notion of `GRACE_EXPIRED`, and an ordinary post-expiry write by a driver whose membership still authorizes them is accepted on arrival. The reason such writes do not arrive is that the client refused to create them, which is Bound 2 and not `F-06`. The claim is the narrower one the paragraph argues: a write by a driver **whose membership no longer authorizes them** fails on arrival regardless of session state, so the argument does not depend on the membership leg of `A-06` left as a `T018` requirement above.

**That narrower claim holds only under a condition this ADR must state rather than assume.** Membership existence, status and role must be resolved from **live server state on every authorized request** and never carried as trusted session claims — required work item 6 below (`T018-1`). Under `D-07 B`’s long session, an implementation that mints company and role into session material at authentication satisfies every other word of this ADR and silently voids both this argument and Consequences item 4 — and nobody would notice, because the document would still say it.

**What the boundary does and does not change.** It **shrinks the population** of operations that can ever reach a terminal rejection — operations queued *before* expiry are in the queue either way, and the boundary does not save them. It does **not** reduce the `OPEN-002` dependency itself: `OPEN-002` is needed exactly as much, and exactly as early, as it was before, and it still governs what happens to every operation that is rejected.

**Two bounds govern the number of days, which is still open, and whoever sets it must weigh both.** They must not be collapsed into one argument.

*Bound 1 — revocation latency.* The window is the maximum latency of revocation on a device that never connects, so a longer window is a longer period in which a revoked device keeps working. The design package makes this bound **conditional on revocation existing at all** (`A-06`) and instructs that `A-06` be confirmed or denied *before* the number is set. **`ADR-019` decides `A-06`.** It is not derived from the package: `05`'s option B *rests on* `A-06` — its own text reads *"Lost devices are individually revocable (**ASSUMPTION** A-06)"* — so citing option B as the source of the discharge would be circular. What happened is stronger. `D-06 B` requires a lost device to be individually revocable, and that option was taken with that property, which makes server-side revocation a **requirement on `T018`** rather than an assumption. Bound 1 is live on that basis. **That discharge covers the device leg only.** `A-06` as written also covers a driver who leaves the company and a suspended `CompanyMember`, and `D-06 B` delivers neither; nothing in this ADR says that suspending a membership terminates a live session. Session termination on membership suspension is therefore itself a **`T018` requirement**, to be settled there together with the departing-driver case. Consequences item 4 and the SIM-swap residual risk both lean on that membership leg. **Bound 1’s revocation latency is also meaningless for the membership case until that leg is filled**, which is why the residual risk below bars setting this number until it is.

*Bound 2 — the grace-expiry write boundary decided above.* It is enforced entirely client-side, which is why **Bound 2 survives `A-06` being false and Bound 1 does not**. With `A-06` now decided and revocation a `T018` requirement, **both bounds are live**. Neither points toward a longer window.

### `D-16` — company-context switch with unsent work

**Who decided this, and when.** `D-16` was **not** part of the original fifteen. It was raised by security review of this ADR — no decision in the fifteen, and nothing in the design package, required the queue to be empty before a driver holding more than one active membership changes company context from `AUTH-12` — put to the programme owner, and **decided by the owner**. It is recorded with its actor for the same reason `D-08`’s fourth property is.

**The reasoning.** This is `D-10 C`’s own principle applied unchanged: queued work belongs to the identity **and the context** that authored it, the safe path is the default and the lossy path is deliberate. `D-10 C` already requires the queue to drain before sign-out, with a destructive confirmation naming the count of unsent items when that is impossible. A context switch unmakes the same promise in the same way, and there was no reason for the two to differ.

**What it closes.** Queued operations carry the **authoring** `company_id` (`F-08`, `F-21`) while a switched session resolves a **different** company context. With no precondition, a legitimate driver with no attacker involved produces one of two outcomes, neither of which anyone had chosen: either the server rejects the queue en masse — a mass terminal failure on work the driver was told was accepted (`F-18`, `F-23`), landing in unresolved `OPEN-002`, at a moment the driver has no reason to connect to the switch — or the server authorizes against the session’s context and accepts payloads tagged with a company the session no longer holds, which is a **cross-tenant write** against `F-07` and `ADR-010`.

**The server-side invariant is separate, and it is not the owner’s to choose.** It is a `T018` requirement: **the server rejects any operation whose payload `company_id` does not match the company context resolved for that request.** That closes the cross-tenant branch regardless of what any client does, and it is recorded as `T018-8` below. `D-16` closes the branch on the client, where the driver can still be told what is happening; the invariant closes it on the server, where it cannot be bypassed. Both are required.

### What this means end to end

A driver never learns, types or resets a password. The office creates their record; they activate one device with their phone number and a one-time code; from then on that device opens with a fingerprint or a PIN. The device works fully offline for a bounded number of days, says honestly when it last reached the office, warns before that window closes, and never destroys accepted work when it does. Every failure route — forgotten PIN, lost handset, invalidated biometric key, exhausted attempts, suspended membership — ends at "call the office and get a new activation code", which is a recovery path a fleet already operates today.

## Consequences

### Unblocked by this decision

- Backend `T017` (OPEN-001 closure) and `T018` (authentication/session) may proceed.
- `T019` (security-critical audit) follows them.
- Android production authentication and device-trust work is no longer gated on `OPEN-001`. It remains gated on `OPEN-002` for anything that queues a real operation.

### Required work this decision creates

1. **`ApiErrorCode` authentication codes (D-14).** Additive per ADR-014's own rule that adding a code is additive and changing a released one is breaking. Without them, five distinct driver situations — wrong credential, session expired, session revoked, rate-limited, membership suspended — collapse into one message covering four different required driver actions. This must become a fact in `T017`, and no later than `T018`. **These five are stage-scoped per `D-14`, and one of them is not an authentication failure at all:** *membership suspended* is a **post-authentication** signal — it is the `EMP` state of item 4, reached by a successful authentication that returns zero active memberships — and it must never appear as a pre-authentication failure code. Surfaced before proof of the activation code it becomes a confirmation oracle for code guessing, in a code space this ADR leaves unbounded (`S-06` sets no length or expiry, `D-11` sets no threshold).
2. **An operator surface.** Three decisions depend on it: D-02 (provisioning), D-06 (device revocation) and D-12 (code re-issuance). **No web implementation repository exists yet.** At pilot, driver provisioning and code re-issuance must therefore happen through a backend-side mechanism. This is a real sequencing dependency between `OPEN-001` and the web lane and is recorded here rather than left implicit. **It is also the security posture of the primary recovery route for the whole pilot, and is stated as a residual risk below rather than left as sequencing alone.**
3. **A written operator verification procedure, to a minimum specification.** Under D-12 the operator route is primary, and under D-03 an operator may read a code aloud. That makes the operator procedure a security control, not an operational convenience — and a control needs a specification, not an intention. It must name: **which factors verify a caller, and which explicitly do not** — the registered phone number does not, because under `D-01 A` it is the identifier itself and under SIM-swap it is the attacker's, and neither do facts printed on a waybill, a truck door or a loading-bay manifest; **who owns the procedure**; and that **it exists in writing before `D-12`'s operator route carries production traffic**. `D-01 D`'s internal reference is the stronger factor, but `05` D-01 option B records that a driver often does not have it to hand, so it cannot silently carry the verification on its own. Every issuance and re-issuance of an activation code, **including the verification performed and the identity of the issuing operator**, must be audited to `F-32`'s shape (`T018-5`/`T019`). A recovery route whose only control is a human judgement that is never recorded cannot be reviewed after an incident, and therefore cannot be improved after one.
4. **The `EMP` state must not present as a credential error.** An identity with zero active memberships authenticates successfully and has nowhere to go, and because `CompanyMember` carries status, a *suspended* driver reaches exactly this state. Showing it as a sign-in failure would send that driver into a retry loop and then into a lockout.
5. **Server-side revocation, and the two legs of `A-06` this ADR does not fill.** `D-06 B` makes per-device revocation a `T018` requirement (see Bound 1 above). Session termination on **membership suspension** and on a **departing driver** are `T018` requirements that `D-06 B` does not deliver. These are stated here as well as under `D-08` because an implementer opening this ADR for the work it creates reads this list, and a requirement filed under an argument about a deferred number is a requirement that will be missed.
6. **Per-request authorization freshness (`T018-1`).** Membership existence, status and role are resolved from **live server state on every authorized request** and are never carried as trusted session claims for the life of a `D-07 B` long session. Two things in this document are false without it: the `D-08` boundary argument above, and item 4 — a suspended driver only reaches `EMP` if membership status is read live at the Company Context stage. The idempotency replay path must re-run authorization before returning a stored outcome (`T018-7`).
7. **The company-context payload invariant (`D-16`, `T018-8`).** The server rejects any operation whose payload `company_id` does not match the company context resolved for that request. `D-16` settles the client behaviour; this invariant is what closes the cross-tenant branch irrespective of the client.

### Requirements routed to `T018`, `T019` and `T083`

Raised by the independent and security review passes on this ADR and recorded here so the task packets inherit them by name. None of them changes what is decided above; each is a statement `T017`, `T018`, `T019` or `T083` would otherwise have to choose for itself.

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| `T018-1` | Membership freshness. `CompanyMember` status and role resolved from live server state on every request; never cached into session material. Required work item 6. |
| `T018-2` | Activation-code binding. The code is bound to the `Driver`/`CompanyMember` it was issued for, validated **as a pair** with the submitted identifier rather than independently, and consumed atomically so that "one-time" survives concurrent submission. Under `D-03` the identifier is not a secret and operator voice delivery sends no SMS, so the code is the whole of the primary proof; validated globally rather than against its issuee, any live code activates against any known number. |
| `T018-3` | `A-05` company context. Expose the active-membership list, and validate the client-selected context against the authenticated identity's active memberships on **every** request, not only at selection. `D-05 C` is the first decision that puts a client-chosen context on the wire. |
| `T018-4` | `A-01` repeat tolerance. An exact resubmission of the activation code does not consume an attempt from `D-11`'s budget. `03` §6 flags this for `T018`; on a flapping link `D-11`'s server-side lockout makes it a real field failure. |
| `T018-5` / `T019` | `A-08` audit. Activation, device registration, device revocation and **operator-mediated code issuance including the identity of the issuing operator and the verification performed** are audited under `F-32`. Required work item 3 declares the operator procedure a security control; an unaudited control cannot be verified after the fact. |
| `T018-6` | For whoever sets `D-11`'s values. The ADR names one scope hazard (a depot behind one IP). The symmetric one: under `D-01 A` the identifier is a phone number anyone may know, so an identifier-scoped lockout is a **targeted denial of service against a named driver** — and out of hours, which is already an accepted residual risk, that driver has no route back. |
| `T018-7` | Idempotency replay re-authorizes. `ADR-008` stores company + operation + `clientRequestId` and returns the prior outcome on exact retry; nothing in `ADR-008`, `F-21` or this ADR says that path re-runs authorization. Without it, a caller whose membership was suspended after the original write replays the key and is served company data they are no longer entitled to. The key does not carry the author (`03` §5.3, `A-07`), so within one company the replay is available to any driver holding another's `clientRequestId`, which `F-22` guarantees is stable. |
| `T018-8` | The company-context payload invariant. Required work item 7 and `D-16`. |
| `T083-1` | The grace window is measured on a clock the device holder controls. Bound 2 survives absence of connectivity, not an adversary holding the handset: rolling the clock back defeats a client-side timer, and with `S-09`'s absolute maximum open there is then no bound at all on an unrevoked device. Anchor the window to a server-issued timestamp plus elapsed-realtime, and treat clock regression as expiry rather than extension. |
| `T083-2` | Capture attribution, a hazard **created by the `D-08` boundary**. At `GRACE_EXPIRED` capture continues but is by definition not a queued operation, so it carries no `company_id`, no `clientRequestId` (`F-08`, `F-21`) and no author until it is attached to a business write after re-authentication. If the device is re-authenticated by a **different identity**, or under a different Company context, that capture attaches to the wrong principal. This is `A-07` in a form the design package did not have, because the boundary this ADR draws creates it. It needs an attribution rule **at capture time**, not at attachment time. |

### Residual risks accepted

- **With no operator verification procedure in force, voice social engineering of the operator route is the cheapest path in.** `D-12` makes that route **primary** and `D-03` lets an operator read a code aloud. One phone call — naming a driver whose name and number are visible on a waybill, a truck door or a loading-bay manifest, asking for a new activation code, and activating a handset — needs no carrier compromise, no cost and no technical step. It is stated first and unconditionally, not as the conditional "*a* weak operator procedure would make this cheapest": until required work item 3 exists there is no procedure at all, so the weakest case is the standing one. `D-06 B` also removes the tell that `D-06 C` would have given: an additional registered device does not displace the driver's own, so the legitimate driver keeps working and nothing surfaces that a takeover happened. `AUTH-12` lists active devices, but only to a driver who thinks to look. That is what taking `D-06 B` costs, recorded rather than an argument to reopen it.
- **SIM-swap is the second path, and less is mitigating it than it appears.** `D-01` (phone number) with `D-13` (SMS) means an attacker who takes over the SIM holds both the identifier and the delivery channel. Neither of the two obvious mitigations survives inspection. The operator voice fallback calls the **registered number** — the very asset the attacker now controls — so voice reaches them exactly as SMS does; it mitigates only if the operator procedure forbids treating control of the registered number as evidence of identity, which is required work item 3's job and does not exist yet. And `D-06` device registration is a revocation **target**, not detection: the office cannot aim a revocation at a device it has no reason to know exists. The honest statement is that a SIM-swap activation under the decided model is **silent**, and that mitigating it depends on detection — a notification on new-device activation, or an operator-visible device inventory. Neither is decided here.
- **The operator route sets the effective strength of the whole scheme.** Under D-12 option C the weaker of the two recovery routes governs. A procedure that verifies a caller by name and date of birth alone makes social engineering trivial — and under `D-01 A` the first factor an operator reaches for, *"what number are you calling about?"*, is the one the attacker holds by construction, under SIM-swap and under plain impersonation alike.
- **At pilot, the primary recovery route runs outside any operator surface.** Required work item 2 records that no web implementation repository exists, so provisioning and code re-issuance happen through a backend-side mechanism. The route `D-12 C` makes primary is therefore, for the whole pilot, direct backend action by engineers — outside whatever procedure item 3 writes, and outside whatever audit an operator surface would have carried.
- **Until both remaining legs of `A-06` are filled, a suspended or departed driver keeps writing.** `D-06 B` aims revocation at a *lost* device; a fired driver's device was never lost, and this ADR names no operator action for the deliberate case. Until `T018` implements session termination on membership suspension and on departure, such a driver retains full write capability on an already-activated device for the session lifetime — `S-09`, unset, and `D-07 B` makes it deliberately long — **plus** the grace window, `D-08`, also unset. Two unset numbers that multiply. Consequences item 4 does not engage against a **live** session, which never re-derives membership; and `02` §5 records that company context is not re-selected on re-authentication, so a device holding a context whose membership has since been suspended keeps that context across re-authentication. **Neither the `D-08` number nor the `S-09` session lifetime may be set before both remaining legs of `A-06` are settled in `T018`.**
- **Out-of-hours recovery is unsolved.** The operator route is primary and the office is not always reachable. The self-service OTP route covers this only when the driver still holds the registered number, which is not the commonest failure.
- **No step-up on financial actions (D-09).** A stolen unlocked handset can file an expense claim. This is accepted because a Driver-role write enters `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → APPROVED|REJECTED` and approval is the control, not authentication. **If the Driver role ever gains a real financial posting capability, D-09 must be reopened.**

### Deliberately left open

These values are not settled here. Each was presented and consciously deferred; none is an oversight. This list is authoritative for what remains open.

- **D-08 — the number of days in the offline grace window.** The design package declines to invent it because it is a business-risk decision. The *shape* is decided and binding, including the grace-expiry write boundary. **Two bounds govern the number and both must be weighed; they are set out under "`D-08` — where the line falls at grace expiry, and the two bounds on the number" in the Decision section above.** Until the number is set, offline-boundary work and `T083` cannot be fully closed. It may not be set at all until both remaining legs of `A-06` are settled in `T018` — see the residual risks above.
- **The numeric policies this ADR does not set.** `S-05` PIN length and composition; `S-06` activation-code length, expiry and resend interval; `S-09` session duration, renewal interval **and absolute maximum lifetime** — under `D-07 B`'s long session the absolute maximum is a distinct value from the renewal interval. The *shapes* are decided above; the values are not, and `07` §6 lists them among its acceptance criteria. They are open on the same terms as `D-08` and `D-11`. `S-09`'s session lifetime carries the same `A-06` bar as the `D-08` number.
- **D-11 — rate-limit scope, threshold, duration and reset.** The shape is decided and binding, including that the remaining time must reach the driver. The values are a security decision. Note that a whole fleet at one depot may share an IP, so an IP-scoped threshold can lock out a depot.

These remain open under `OPEN-001`'s successor scope and must be recorded as such in `ai/DECISIONS_INDEX.md`. They do not reopen the fifteen decisions above.

### Not covered

`OPEN-002` (Android sync terminal-error policy) is untouched by this ADR and remains open. D-10 borders on it — retaining a queue against a previous identity is squarely `OPEN-002` territory, which is one reason option D was not chosen — but this ADR neither closes nor prejudges it. `D-08`'s grace-expiry write boundary **shrinks the population** of operations that can ever reach a terminal rejection. It does not reduce the `OPEN-002` dependency, which is needed exactly as much and exactly as early as before, and which still governs what happens to every operation that is rejected.

## Alternatives considered

The full option set, with field and security costs for each of the fifteen sub-decisions, is in `ai/design/mobile/auth/05-open-001-decision-alternatives.md`, and the designer's proposed combination with its reasoning is in `ai/design/mobile/auth/06-recommendation.md`. Both were reviewed sub-decision by sub-decision before this ADR was written. The decision taken matches the designer's recommended combination on all fifteen.

Two points where the package corrected itself are preserved here rather than smoothed over, because they bear on how firm each choice is:

- **D-04** is `DERIVED`, not platform-forced. Platform facts `PF-02` (no sensor or enrolment) and `PF-03` (enrolment change) rule out biometric-only and OS-credential-only. They do **not** rule out PIN-only, which survives them equally. Ranking biometric-with-PIN-fallback above PIN-only is a judgement about typing cost.
- **D-10** is `DERIVED`. `FACT` F-23 rules out only option A (clear everything always). Options B, C and D all satisfy it; choosing C is a judgement that the safe path should be the default and the lossy path deliberate.
